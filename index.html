Professional Self-Assessment

Throughout my Computer Science program at Southern New Hampshire University, I have been able to build on my professional IT experience while developing new skills in programming, software development, databases, and problem solving. Before moving into IT and systems support, I spent several years in warehouse operations. Having experience on both sides has given me a better understanding of how technology affects everyday operations and the people who rely on it. My goal is to move further into software engineering, with a long term interest in site reliability.

In my current role, I am part of a small IT team supporting a distribution center with approximately 800 to 900 employees across four shifts. My responsibilities include troubleshooting equipment and software, managing user access, and working with associates, management, and outside support teams to resolve issues. This experience has taught me the importance of teamwork, communication, and being able to explain technical problems to people with different levels of experience. I have also started applying my programming skills at work by developing an Excel VBA tool to improve a process in our Breakpack department. Although it is still in development, the project has allowed me to combine my operational knowledge with what I have learned through my coursework.

Throughout my degree, I have worked with several programming languages, databases, and development tools. Outside of my capstone artifacts, one project that stands out from my coursework is Travlr Getaways, a full stack web application built using the MEAN stack. The project included a public facing travel website, an administrative interface, and a REST API. It helped me better understand how different parts of an application work together and how important organization and maintainability are in software development. My coursework in data structures and algorithms also changed how I approach programming. Rather than focusing only on whether code works, I have learned to consider efficiency, performance, and choosing the right approach for the problem. I have also gained experience with SQL, MongoDB, and Power BI, which strengthened my understanding of working with and analyzing data.

Security has also become an important part of how I think about software and systems. In my current job, I regularly work with user access and permissions, so I understand the importance of protecting systems and information. My coursework has helped me build on that experience by introducing secure coding practices, input validation, authentication, and the importance of identifying potential vulnerabilities before they become bigger problems. These are skills I want to continue developing as I move into software engineering.

Developing my ePortfolio has given me an opportunity to look back at earlier projects and see how my skills have grown. My three selected artifacts represent different areas of computer science. The GoalWeight Android application focuses on software design and engineering, where I improved the application's structure, security, and validation. My ABCU Course Advising Program demonstrates my understanding of algorithms and data structures through the implementation of an AVL tree and prerequisite cycle detection. During testing with seven courses inserted in ascending order, the AVL tree maintained a height of three, compared to the height of seven that the original binary search tree would have produced with the same data. Finally, the Grazioso Salvare Animal Shelter Dashboard highlights my database skills through improvements to CRUD operations, data validation, and the dashboard itself. Each enhancement allowed me to revisit an existing project, identify areas for improvement, and apply what I have learned since originally creating it. Together, these projects demonstrate my ability to work with different technologies, solve problems, and improve existing software. Completing this program has helped me become more confident in my abilities and better prepared to continue growing professionally in the computer science field.

ABCU Course Advising Program

Algorithms & Data Structures

The ABCU Course Advising Program is a C++ application that loads and organizes course information, displays courses in alphanumeric order, and allows users to search for individual courses and their prerequisites. I originally created the program for my Data Structures and Algorithms course and later enhanced it for my Computer Science capstone.

The enhancement replaced the original binary search tree with a self balancing AVL tree and added depth first search to detect cycles within prerequisite relationships. I also improved file loading and expanded testing to evaluate tree balancing, course searches, and prerequisite validation.
